# Reference Style Calibration

Use this when the user asks to follow a reference draft, PDF, deck, page range, previous output, or specific口径. The goal is to match the useful expression logic of the reference, not to copy its words.

## Classify The Reference Intent

Identify what the user wants to borrow:

- **Logic**: argument chain, decision sequence, or method.
- **Structure**: section order, page roles, tables, or information density.
- **Register**: professional level, authority, distance from the reader, and industry vocabulary.
- **Rhythm**: title length, paragraph pace, sentence density, and use of tables or short judgments.
- **Wording**: recurring phrase patterns, diction, and degree of directness.
- **Visual organization**: relationship between titles, diagrams, tables, captions, and supporting copy.

Treat "参考思路" as logic or structure unless the user also asks to match the expression. Treat "参考口径/表达/语气" as register, rhythm, or wording. When the request is ambiguous, infer from the current complaint and artifact; do not silently copy all dimensions.

## Apply The Relevant Reference Decisions

Use the requested dimensions and current complaint to select what matters. Keep the internal comparison proportionate to the task.

For structure or logic, trace what the reader knows or wonders before a passage, what it supplies, and why the next passage follows. For an ad, “痛点、卖点、行动入口” names roles; inspect which need the example establishes, how its actual provision answers that need, and what action the reader can now consider. Rebuild those relationships with the current material rather than filling the same number of paragraphs or copying their openings. A reference can expose that the current draft is performing the wrong kind of task; adding its title or ending to the old draft will not fix that mismatch.

For register, rhythm, or wording, select the choices that affect this reader: speaking authority and distance, sentence movement, useful vocabulary, and degree of directness. Preserve intentional differences between page or passage roles. Do not output the comparison unless rationale is requested.

For a personal voice, ground each useful observation in a short excerpt or passage location and explain what that passage does: for example, revises an earlier belief, compares alternatives, or leaves a difficulty unresolved. Record relevant counterexamples instead of turning a few samples into universal habits. Use only the traits the material supports; do not impose a fixed count. Do not copy whole reference articles into the profile.

Compare the rewrite with the reference at the paragraph level. Ask whether its example supplies information needed for the judgment and whether its ending performs a similar role when appropriate. Matching sentence length or inserting “我认为” is not enough. Current user feedback overrides an inferred style habit, even when the reference author sometimes uses that habit.

## What To Preserve

Preserve structures demonstrated by the reference when they fit the current genre. The following are options for strategy decks and decision-oriented reports, not defaults for personal articles:

- title as page judgment, not category label
- opening sentence as strategic conclusion
- table as decision tool, not decoration
- cross-page logic, such as target -> strategy -> execution -> node example -> search/content -> acceptance
- professional terms when they name real planning objects

Personal articles may open with an observation, an event, a question, or a judgment, and may end without a summary or instruction. Choose from the actual material and requested voice rather than importing a report's conclusion-first structure.

## What Not To Copy

- Do not copy factual claims, numbers, brand details, or internal口径 from the reference unless they belong to the current task.
- Do not imitate decorative jargon if the reference itself is weak.
- Do not make all genres sound like the reference. Apply it only to the requested scene.

## Reference Quality Filter

Do not treat every feature of a reference as desirable. Separate:

- useful professional conventions worth preserving
- project-specific facts that must not transfer
- weak jargon or unsupported certainty that should not be copied
- visual or structural habits that work only because of the reference's content density

When the reference contains both strong structure and weak language, borrow the structure and rewrite the language.

## Mixed-Register Artifacts

One document may contain more than one local register. An executive opening, strategy map, execution table, budget page, and appendix should feel related, but they should not use identical sentence density or vocabulary. Calibrate the relevant choices per page family when the artifact mixes strategic, operational, analytical, and evidentiary sections.

## Failure Modes

- **Over-cleaning**: the draft becomes clearer but loses the professional register the user wanted.
- **Partial borrowing**: only a few words are copied, while page logic and rhythm remain unchanged.
- **Scene mismatch**: a strategy deck becomes a project memo, or a public whitepaper becomes an internal report.
- **Reference leakage**: old brand names, metrics, or internal assumptions enter the new draft.

## Quick Check

Before final output, ask:

- Does this sound like the same type of artifact as the reference?
- Are the page roles aligned, not just the vocabulary?
- Did any useful professional term get flattened into casual language?
- Did any reference-only fact leak into the new draft?
